# Good vs bad practices

Concrete examples from **Chapter 2 — AI Instructions**.

---

## Example 1: Testing a sorting function

**Good practice**  
- You ask AI: “How do I test a sorting function?”  
- It gives you ideas. You try them and **review the results with a peer**.  
- You refine the approach together.

**Bad practice**  
- You ask AI to write the whole function, copy-paste it into your activity.  
- In peer-evaluation you **can’t explain what it does or why**.  
- You lose credibility — and fail the activity.

---

## Example 2: Parser design

**Good practice**  
- You use AI to help **design** a parser.  
- You **walk through the logic with a peer**.  
- You catch two bugs and rewrite it together — better, cleaner, and fully understood.

**Bad practice**  
- You let Copilot generate the code for a key part of your activity.  
- It compiles, but you **can’t explain how it handles pipes**.  
- During the evaluation you fail to justify — and fail the activity.

---

## Pattern to keep

| Do | Don’t |
|----|--------|
| Use AI for ideas, structure, or small steps | Use AI to produce final answers you can’t explain |
| Test and question every output | Copy-paste without testing or understanding |
| Review and refine with a peer | Rely only on your own validation |
| Build something you can justify in an eval | Hand in code you don’t understand |

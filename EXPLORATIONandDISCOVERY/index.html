Chapter 2 — AI Instructions. Use AI to reduce repetitive tasks, develop prompting skills, and only use what you understand and can take responsibility for.

Main message

  • Use AI to reduce repetitive or tedious tasks.
  • Develop prompting skills — both coding and non-coding — that will benefit your future career.
  • Learn how AI systems work to better anticipate and avoid common risks, biases, and ethical issues.
  • Continue building both technical and power skills by working with your peers.
  • Only use AI-generated content that you fully understand and can take responsibility for.

Learner rules

  • Take time to explore AI tools and understand how they work, so you can use them ethically and reduce potential biases.
  • Reflect on your problem before prompting — clearer, more detailed, and more relevant prompts.
  • Systematically check, review, question, and test anything generated by AI.
  • Always seek peer review — don’t rely solely on your own validation.

Reflection before prompting

Use this checklist before you send a prompt. Persistence is stored in this browser only.

  • Clarify the problem
  • Scope the request
  • Plan for validation

Good vs bad practices

Testing a sorting function

Good practice

Ask AI: “How do I test a sorting function?” Get ideas, try them, and review the results with a peer. Refine the approach together.

Bad practice

Ask AI to write the whole function, copy-paste into your activity. In peer-evaluation you can’t explain what it does or why — you lose credibility and fail the activity.

Parser design

Good practice

Use AI to help design a parser. Walk through the logic with a peer. Catch bugs and rewrite together — better, cleaner, and fully understood.

Bad practice

Let Copilot generate the code for a key part. It compiles, but you can’t explain how it handles pipes. During evaluation you fail to justify — and fail the activity.

Pattern: Use AI for ideas and small steps; test and question everything; review with a peer. Only use output you understand and can justify.

Prompt starters (explore & refine)

Use these as starting points; always add your context and constraints. Then test the output and review with a peer.

  • “How do I test [X]?” (e.g. a sorting function, a parser, an API call)
  • “What’s a simple way to [specific small task] in [language]?”
  • “Explain [concept] in one paragraph, then give a minimal code example.”
  • “What are the edge cases for [algorithm/function]?”
  • “Help me design the structure for [X]; I’ll implement the details.”

Phase outcomes

  • Develop both general-purpose and domain-specific prompting skills.
  • Boost productivity with effective use of AI tools.
  • Strengthen computational thinking, problem-solving, adaptability, and collaboration.
